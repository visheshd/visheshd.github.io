Should You Care About startup hiring events? Only If You're Serious About roadmap-critical engineering with fewer people

June 6, 2024
startup hiring

Startup hiring events are back.

Speed dating for talent.
Fancy booths. Branded T-shirts.
And every startup shouting:

"We're hiring engineers!"

But here's the truth:
Most early-stage startups shouldn't be hiring engineers at all.

Not yet.

What No One Tells You at Startup Hiring Events

These events are designed for volume.

High-funnel hiring. Lots of conversations.
Little context. Zero filtering.

Which is great if you've raised $20M and need to build a team.

But if you're early stage, still figuring things out?

You'll leave with a stack of resumes and no idea what to do next.

The Hidden Cost of Hiring Too Early

Let's say you meet a few good devs at an event.
You interview, you hire, and suddenly…

  • You're managing people instead of building.
  • Your roadmap is unclear but now has deadlines.
  • Your burn rate just tripled.
  • And the thing you should be building still isn't live.

You've added team before clarity.
That's how startups slow down before they ever speed up.

So... Should You Go?

Yes — if you're clear about what you're looking for:

✅ You've validated the problem
✅ You know what needs to be built next
✅ You're hiring for very specific execution needs
✅ You have a culture of async, fast-moving work
✅ You're not afraid to say "No" to most candidates

Otherwise?

Save the swag budget.

And focus on roadmap-critical engineering — with fewer, sharper people.

What to Do Instead

If you're still figuring out what to build, or how to grow —
Don't build a hiring funnel. Build momentum.

Here's a better playbook:

  • Work with a former founder who can build across product, tech, and growth.
  • Use AI to automate internal tools before adding headcount.
  • Scope down. Ship fast.
  • Let execution inform your hiring — not the other way around.

TL;DR

Startup hiring events make sense when your only problem is capacity.

But most early-stage startups have a clarity problem.

If you care about roadmap-critical engineering with fewer people — skip the noise.

Focus on what moves the needle.
