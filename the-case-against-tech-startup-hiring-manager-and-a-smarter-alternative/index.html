The Case Against tech startup hiring manager (And a Smarter Alternative)

June 18, 2024
startup hiring

In big tech, a hiring manager makes perfect sense.

They coordinate interviews, manage pipelines, and help scale teams across functions.

But at an early-stage startup?

A hiring manager is often a very expensive way to stay stuck.

Here's why bringing in a hiring manager too early is a mistake — and what to do instead.

The Hidden Cost of a Hiring Manager

Early-stage founders bring on hiring managers to "buy back time."
But what they often get is:

  • A flood of candidates with unclear job descriptions
  • Roles filled before roles are even scoped
  • Pressure to grow headcount over outcomes

You start managing process instead of solving problems.

What You Actually Need: High-Agency Execution

You don't need a hiring pipeline — you need a shipping pipeline.

At early stage, the best hire isn't someone who manages other hires.

It's someone who:

  • Turns messy ideas into working systems
  • Doesn't wait for perfect specs
  • Owns outcomes end-to-end
  • Has built under pressure before

You know who fits that profile?

A former founder.

Why Former Founders Work Better Than Hiring Managers (Early On)

Former founders bring:

✅ Context across product, tech, and growth
✅ Judgment to say "this isn't worth building"
✅ Speed to unblock without handholding
✅ Outcome mindset — not process mindset

They don't manage a team. They act like one.

Still Need to Hire? Here's a Smarter Flow

If you really do need to hire, try this first:

1. Define the job by outcome, not title

"We need someone to increase demo-to-signup conversion"
Not:
"We need a growth hacker"

2. Use an auto-reply filter

Ask every applicant to reply with a 2-min Loom or 3-line solution to a real problem.
9 out of 10 won't reply — and that's your filter.

3. Build internal tools to delay non-critical hires

Before hiring ops, support, or analytics — try AI + automation first.

TL;DR

Hiring managers make sense after you've got repeatable systems.

Before that?

They just add meetings and resumes.

If you want to move faster with fewer people, the better move is:

  • Work with a former founder
  • Build internal tools
  • Hire only when outcomes are bottlenecked by bandwidth — not clarity
