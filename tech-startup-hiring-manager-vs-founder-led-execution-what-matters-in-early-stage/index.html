Tech Startup Hiring Manager vs Founder-Led Execution: What Matters in Early Stage

June 3, 2024
startup hiring

In the early days of a startup, every decision compounds.

But one of the most expensive decisions — in both time and money — is this:

Do we bring in a hiring manager or keep executing founder-led?

At first glance, it sounds responsible. Structured. "Let's hire a hiring manager to scale the team."

But here's the truth:
At early stage, you don't need a team.
You need traction.

What Hiring Managers Optimize For

A hiring manager — internal or outsourced — is wired to:

  • Fill seats
  • Shortlist candidates
  • Run structured interviews
  • Help you scale

Sounds great… if you already know what you need.

But most early-stage founders don't.

You're still figuring out:

  • What to build
  • Who it's for
  • What's working

And no hiring manager can answer that for you.

What Founder-Led Execution Optimizes For

Founder-led execution is messier. But it's real.

It's about:

  • Writing the first line of code
  • Shipping v0.1 of the product
  • Talking to the first 10 users
  • Rewriting the pitch for the 5th time
  • Running tiny experiments across product and growth

It's not scalable — yet. But it's where insight lives.

It's where leverage comes from.

The Founder Advantage

A founder who executes has superpowers:

  • They skip the job req and go straight to the solution
  • They don't need permission to change direction
  • They don't spend two weeks onboarding — they just build

You don't need a hiring pipeline.
You need a shipping pipeline.

When to Pause and Hire

Bring in a hiring manager when:

  • You've hit product-market fit
  • Your bottleneck is people, not clarity
  • You have repeatable tasks and well-scoped roles
  • You're ready to scale, not just search

Until then, the best "hiring manager" is a founder who builds.

Or a former founder who's done it enough times to move like one.

Final Thought

At early stage, hiring for hiring's sake is a trap.

If you're not careful, you'll hire a manager to help you hire another manager.

Instead:

  • Fix what's broken.
  • Ship what matters.
  • Automate what's repetitive.
  • Bring in leverage, not overhead.
